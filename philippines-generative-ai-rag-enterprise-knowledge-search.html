Philippine Companies Using Generative AI and RAG: Enterprise Knowledge Search Case Studies

John Christian YongcoOctober 7, 202612 min read

TL;DR

Philippine companies are trying generative AI, but few have turned it into a dependable company system. The useful next step is not another generic chatbot. It is a knowledge search tool that lets staff or customers ask questions and get answers based on approved company documents, policies, product details, and systems.

Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, supports this kind of work. It searches selected company sources before it answers. That gives users more current, traceable answers than a public AI tool working from general information alone.

Globe offers a public local example. It has given employees access to AI tools and an internal RAG toolkit, which employees have used to build hundreds of bots and copilots.

AI Use in Philippine Companies Has Grown Fast

Generative AI is no longer limited to small tests or tech teams. Many employees use tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot to draft content, summarize documents, research topics, write code, and reply to customers.

The bigger question is whether companies can use AI with their own data in a safe and useful way.

The 2025 Philippine AI Report found that 92% of surveyed organizations had used AI in some form. Only 12% had scaled it across the business. That gap shows the difference between trying a tool and building a process people can rely on.

A team may use AI every day but still struggle to find the latest policy, price sheet, proposal, project file, or customer record.

Why Enterprise Knowledge Search Matters

Most companies do not lack information. They struggle to find the right information at the right time.

Important files are often spread across:

  • Google Drive, SharePoint, Dropbox, and local folders
  • PDFs, proposals, manuals, price sheets, and presentations
  • Slack, Teams, Viber, WhatsApp, and email
  • CRM, ERP, HR, and ticketing systems
  • Individual employees' memories

When someone asks, “What is our approved refund policy?” or “Which price sheet is current?” the answer may exist somewhere. Finding the right version can still take too long.

Enterprise knowledge search changes that process. A user can ask a question in normal language and receive an answer with a link or citation to the source document.

What RAG Means in Plain English

RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation.

A RAG system searches selected company files before it writes an answer. It finds relevant information from those files and uses it as the basis for its response.

A public AI tool gives an answer from broad training data. A RAG system can answer from a company's own approved documents.

That does not mean every answer will be correct. The company still needs clean source files, access controls, testing, and someone responsible for keeping information current. But RAG gives the business a clearer way to check where an answer came from.

Case Study: Globe's Internal RAG Toolkit

Globe has shared one of the strongest public examples of enterprise AI use in the Philippines.

The company says its employees can use Gemini for Workspace, ChatGPT Enterprise, and an internal RAG toolkit. These tools have helped employees create more than 400 bots and copilots for daily work. Globe also said its GenAI Quality Audit replaced manual quality checks and reduced monthly costs from millions of pesos to around ₱2,000.

The point is not that every company needs hundreds of bots.

The point is that AI becomes more useful when it is tied to a real task, trusted company data, and a clear person or team responsible for the result.

Where Philippine Companies Can Use RAG

Customer Support

A support assistant can answer common questions about products, warranty terms, delivery, billing, bookings, and service processes.

It should answer only from approved support content. When the system is unsure, it should pass the question to a human agent rather than make up an answer.

Internal Policies and SOPs

HR, operations, finance, and compliance teams receive the same questions many times. Employees ask about leave, benefits, reimbursements, procurement, onboarding, and internal rules.

A knowledge assistant can point people to the correct policy and the exact source document.

Sales and Technical Support

Sales teams need current details about products, prices, warranties, specifications, and case studies. Technical teams need manuals, service records, troubleshooting guides, and installation instructions.

A RAG system can reduce the time spent searching folders or asking senior staff to confirm basic information.

Document Review and Approvals

Many companies still handle forms, invoices, applications, proposals, and approvals through email threads, spreadsheets, and PDFs.

AI can help extract details, summarize documents, spot missing fields, and route requests. People should still make final decisions when there is financial, legal, or compliance risk.

Property, Project, and Location Questions

For property developers, engineering firms, renewable-energy teams, and multi-branch businesses, information is often linked to a location.

An assistant can answer questions such as:

  • “Which projects are near this transport hub?”
  • “What are the current details for this property?”
  • “Which reports apply to this site?”
  • “Find the latest approved brochure and price sheet.”

This is useful when a company needs to understand both documents and physical locations.

Why AI Projects Fail to Grow

Most AI projects do not fail because the model is weak. They fail because the business problem, source data, or ownership is unclear.

Here are common reasons:

No specific problem

“We need AI” is too vague. “Reduce repeated HR policy questions by half” gives the project a clear purpose.

Messy source files

Old PDFs, duplicate documents, conflicting policies, and unclear file ownership lead to weak answers.

Missing access controls

Not every employee should see HR, financial, customer, or legal information. Permissions need to be part of the design from the start.

No quality checks

Teams need to test real questions, review answers, inspect citations, and track questions the system cannot answer.

Too much automation too soon

Start with knowledge search and human review. Give the system permission to take actions only after it performs well in a controlled setting.

How to Assess a RAG Project

Before choosing a vendor or building a system, ask these questions.

Does it solve a repeated and costly problem?

Good first projects deal with questions that staff or customers ask often and that take time to answer.

Are the source documents current?

The business needs clear owners for policies, product information, and project files. AI cannot fix a source library that nobody maintains.

Can users check the source?

For important answers, users should be able to open the policy, manual, price sheet, or other document behind the answer.

Does the system respect permissions?

A sales employee, HR manager, customer, and executive should not automatically see the same information.

How will success be measured?

Useful measures include:

  • Less time spent searching for information
  • Faster first responses to customers
  • Fewer repeated support questions
  • Faster employee onboarding
  • Better answer accuracy
  • Fewer questions escalated to senior staff

A 90-Day Plan for a First RAG Project

Days 1 to 30: Choose one problem and clean the source files

Pick one team or workflow. Gather the relevant documents, remove old versions, identify source owners, and list the questions the system should answer.

Days 31 to 60: Run a small pilot

Give a limited group access to the assistant. Test real questions, check the sources it retrieves, and record what it cannot answer.

Days 61 to 90: Review results before expanding

Look at usage, answer quality, time saved, and failure cases. Set permissions and escalation rules. Then decide whether to expand the system to another team or use case.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is enterprise knowledge search?

Enterprise knowledge search helps staff find company information across documents, systems, and approved data sources. Users can ask questions in normal language instead of relying only on folder names and keywords.

How is RAG different from fine-tuning?

RAG retrieves information when the user asks a question. Fine-tuning changes a model's behavior through training examples. RAG is often a better fit for company policies, product documents, and project files because those sources change over time.

Can RAG read spreadsheets and databases?

Yes, but spreadsheets and databases need careful handling. The system should preserve columns, dates, filters, permissions, and calculation logic instead of treating every file as plain text.

Is generative AI safe for Philippine businesses?

It can be used safely with approved data sources, role-based access, audit logs, human review, and clear rules for sensitive information. Companies must also consider their duties under the Philippine Data Privacy Act.

Which industries benefit most from RAG?

RAG works best for organizations with many documents, repeated questions, technical information, detailed policies, or multiple projects and locations. This includes BPOs, telecoms, healthcare, finance, property, logistics, engineering, education, and professional services.

Start With One Useful Knowledge Problem

The Philippine market is moving from casual AI use toward connected business workflows. The companies that benefit most will not be the ones with the most chatbots. They will be the ones that make trusted information easy to find and check.

Start with one department, one knowledge problem, and one measurable result. That is how a RAG project becomes part of daily work instead of another short-term test.
